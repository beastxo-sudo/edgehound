🎯 60-Second Sniper Lab bet $10 each window — do we come out ahead?
The test in plain terms: 60 seconds before each 5-minute Bitcoin window closes, the bot bets $10 on whichever direction the price is currently moving, at the real Polymarket price. Then it checks if that bet won. The scorecard below shows exactly what you'd have if you'd done this every time: how much you put in, how much came back, and your % return. If "every $10 returns more than $10," there's an edge. If less, there isn't. We moved from 5s → 60s because at 5s the price was already 98¢ (no room for profit); 60s out it should be cheaper. Every bet is settled against Polymarket itself — the actual market that pays the bet, read after it officially resolves (shown as ⬤ Polymarket). Binance and Coinbase are kept as a cross-check; if they ever disagree with Polymarket's result, the bet still counts as Polymarket scored it (that's what your money would do) but the disagreement is flagged. Bets that close exactly flat, or can't be confirmed, are excluded rather than guessed.

📊 Order-Book Imbalance Lab does liquidity lead price, or is that thread hype?
The hypothesis (from the viral thread): when order-book depth piles up on one side while price stays flat, price follows seconds later — "bots see liquidity, retail sees price." We test it directly: snapshot the book, measure which side is heavier, wait ${'8'} seconds, and check if price moved that way. The honest part is the baseline: we also measure how often price follows a balanced book. If a lopsided book doesn't predict better than a balanced one, the signal is noise. And we flag whether the move even beat the spread you'd have to cross — a real lead that's smaller than the spread is untradeable.
